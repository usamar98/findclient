## What should I read before writing a proposal?

Read the entire post, the latest replies, and the instructions for applying. Find the sentence that describes the desired change in the buyer's own terms. If the author only wants community advice, answer the question within the rules and leave the commercial discussion for a genuine invitation.

Imagine a freelance editor finds this fictional brief. A language teacher has recorded four lessons for a paid course. The audio seems quieter in two recordings, learners need captions, and the teacher wants to release one lesson next Friday. The teacher asks for a quote for the first lesson and one link to similar work. They do not ask for a channel strategy or a new intro sequence.

Write down three facts from the brief before opening a proposal template. Here they are the number of existing recordings, the audio inconsistency, and the deadline for one release. Then list the two missing details that affect the price most. Source runtime and the availability of a corrected transcript probably matter more than how many people subscribe to the teacher's mailing list. These notes will keep your response grounded when you begin drafting.

## How can I offer something useful in the first reply?

Give a brief diagnostic step the buyer can carry out or understand before hiring you. Explain how the result affects your approach. This demonstrates judgment and lets the buyer learn something even if they choose a different freelancer.

For the course, try this opening. “Play a short sentence from each recording at the same device volume. If the voice changes level inside a single recording as well as between lessons, I would allow time for individual adjustments before the final level check. Do you have the original audio track and the transcript for the first lesson?” The reply acknowledges the stated problem and explains one pricing dependency without pretending you have heard the files.

For a web design brief, a useful first check might be viewing one affected page at the exact screen width the buyer mentions. For a writing brief, it could be asking which question the page must help a reader answer. For a data migration, it could be identifying a representative record that must survive the migration intact. Choose an observation specific enough to change your proposed work.

## How should I define the first deliverable?

Describe what will exist at the end of the engagement, who will review it, and how they will decide whether it is ready. Choose a milestone small enough that missing information can be discovered before the entire budget is committed.

A first lesson deliverable could include one edited video, a reviewed caption file, and one revision based on consolidated feedback. Acceptance could mean that the teacher can preview the lesson on the intended course platform, hear speech consistently across the named segments, and toggle captions with the correct timing and spelling. The teacher can evaluate those criteria without knowing which editing software you use.

Separate the pilot lesson from the other three. The buyer has only requested a quote for one release. A successful pilot might establish a repeatable workflow, but the remaining recordings could have different audio quality, lengths, or teaching styles. A sentence reserving the option to price the rest after the pilot is sufficient. List a release deadline only after confirming when files and feedback will arrive.

## How do I talk about price when the scope is uncertain?

Provide a fixed quote only when you can name the assumptions that support it. Otherwise give a bounded paid investigation, a unit rate, or an estimate with a clear trigger for revising it. Explain the information you need to turn the estimate into a commitment.

Suppose the teacher says lesson one has twenty minutes of source footage, one speaker, and a corrected transcript. You can estimate ingest and review time, speech edits, caption alignment, export, and one revision. Add review time for the buyer's platform and allow for client communication. Your price should cover your actual process. A figure copied from another editor's listing cannot tell you whether this project fits your week.

If you quote by hour, describe how the buyer will see progress and when you will pause for approval. If you quote by milestone, define what counts as a change. Replacing the source recording after the approved cut can reset much of the work. Correcting a spelling error in titles often does not. State the distinction using examples suited to the project and agree on it before starting.

## What does a complete forum proposal sound like?

A short proposal should let the client picture the result and make one decision. Use the required application channel and length, and include only the evidence requested. A hiring community can set its own rules on bids and portfolios. [Example hiring guidelines from r/DesignJobs](https://www.reddit.com/r/DesignJobs/comments/c6iy7w/updated_rules_guidelines_read_before_posting/)

Here is an illustrative reply for the teacher. “I can prepare your first course lesson for Friday's release. I would start by checking whether the volume difference happens within the lesson or only between recordings. For the pilot I would deliver an edited lesson, timed captions, and a preview you can play in your course platform. One round of feedback can cover cuts and caption spelling. Could you share the source duration and tell me whether a corrected transcript is available? With those two details I can confirm a fixed price and a first-preview date.”

The teacher requested one work sample. Add a real, permission-cleared link to a lesson you edited and name your role in it. Include a timestamp for the transition or caption treatment worth reviewing. If your only sample is self-directed, label it as a demonstration. A relevant demonstration is more credible than implying that a recognizable brand hired you for work you never delivered.

## How should I review an AI-generated proposal?

Check every sentence against the post, your actual experience, and the terms you can offer. Replace invented qualifications and unsupported diagnoses with verifiable details or a focused question. Read the response as though you were the person responsible for paying the invoice.

Look first for claims such as “I reviewed your store,” “I have fixed this exact problem,” or “I can guarantee the campaign will convert.” These claims require evidence. Remove them when the only input is a public post. An AI can generate fluent technical language without having inspected the underlying work. A useful revision names what you would examine and what evidence would support the diagnosis.

Next check the order. Many drafts begin with a biography and bury the proposed work in the last sentence. Move the first practical action near the top. Cut skills that the buyer did not ask for. Confirm that any price, link, availability date, and timeline came from you. Read the final answer aloud. If it sounds like a brochure you could send to fifty different people, return to the specific symptom and deadline in the brief.

## How should I follow up after submitting?

Follow the buyer's instructions and record what you promised. If the buyer invited a follow-up on a specific day, use that day and add information that helps them decide. When a thread is closed or the buyer declines, mark the opportunity accordingly.

Suppose the teacher replies with a twelve-minute recording and no transcript. That answer changes your estimate. Explain the work required for the first transcript, indicate how the teacher will confirm subject-specific spellings, and update your delivery date. If the teacher sends a corrected transcript later, explain whether it reduces the remaining work. A clear adjustment helps the buyer understand the relationship between the quote and the actual inputs.

After the decision, keep a small debrief. Record which detail got a useful answer, which assumption changed, and how the final scope differed from the post. Review a few debriefs before writing your next template. A pattern of missing source runtimes might justify asking about runtime sooner. A pattern of unanswered generic introductions might justify opening with a better first check.
