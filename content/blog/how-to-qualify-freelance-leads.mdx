## How do you tell a buyer from someone asking for advice?

Consider these two illustrative posts from store owners with the same problem. “Why does the wrong photo appear when I pick a size?” invites troubleshooting. “Looking to hire a Shopify developer to fix our variant photos before Friday. Please send a quote” invites a commercial reply. The broken feature is identical. The second author tells you how they want to engage.

| Check | Evidence in the hiring post | What you still need to ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scope | The issue concerns variant photos | Which product and theme reproduce it? |
| Intent | “Looking to hire” and “send a quote” | Who will approve the fix? |
| Budget | The buyer requests a quote | What price range can they approve? |
| Timing | “Before Friday” | When must the fix be tested and ready to publish? |

## What should the first reply say?

Answer one part of the brief and ask for what you need to scope the rest. For the hiring example, you could write: “Could you share one affected product and the steps that show the wrong photo? I can use those to check whether the variant mapping or the theme is responsible, then quote the fix and confirm whether Friday works.”

That reply gives the merchant a concrete next step. For the advice post, a better opening is a diagnostic question about whether the correct image is assigned to each variant in the product editor. Check the thread’s rules and the author’s preferred contact method before suggesting paid work.

## How much weight should you give a lead score?

Use a score to decide which thread to read first. A stated budget and an established store are useful clues. Neither tells you whether the author can approve your quote. A buyer with a new account may still have a well-defined project. Give more weight to what the person can confirm about this job.

Lead To Client distinguishes a request’s fit for your service from buyer signals such as budget, urgency and business context. When a score and a recent comment disagree, read the comment. A buyer who has already hired someone has answered the most important question. [See how lead scoring works](/how-it-works).

## When should you move the lead into your pipeline?

Record the source and the date of your first reply. Once the buyer confirms scope and invites a quote, write down the deliverable, who signs off and the agreed next contact date. A proposal amount belongs in your pipeline as a possible deal. Mark it won when the buyer accepts. This keeps your follow-up list useful and your revenue totals meaningful.
